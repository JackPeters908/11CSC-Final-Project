From a Catholic perspective, every person has value simply because they exist. In today’s online world, it is easy to forget that behind every screen is a real human being, not just a profile picture or a target for mean comments. Having a social duty means carrying kindness into digital spaces by standing up to cyberbullying, avoiding online drama, and sharing things that actually help others. Ultimately, technology should be used to bring people together, look out for the vulnerable, and build up our real-world communities.

Growing up in New Zealand, I value the real-world connections that shape our communities, whether on the sports field, at school, or with family. Maintaining digital wellbeing is essential because online spaces can easily consume our time and distort how we view ourselves and others. Pope Francis captures this challenge well by reminding us that "happiness is not an app you can download" and warning against the alienation of living in an "unreal world." His call to guard against digital toxicity aligns directly with a Catholic commitment to protecting human dignity. Social media offers great ways to stay connected, but when scrolling replaces face-to-face relationships or fosters online negativity, it pulls us away from authentic community. For me, balancing technology means intentionally using digital tools to build people up, while staying grounded in the physical world and the real relationships that matter most.
